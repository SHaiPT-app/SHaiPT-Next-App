# Hook rubric — the gate

The predecessor channel (medAI Times) published 5–7 times a week for four
months and finished under 500 subscribers. Volume was the strategy and the
strategy failed. Nothing about this pipeline's cadence is inherited.

**This channel ships 2–3 Shorts a week, and the hook is the gate.** A week
with one excellent Short beats a week with three adequate ones. A week with
zero is acceptable and is not a failure state — it means the gate worked.

You must generate **three** candidate hooks, score each against the rubric
below, and recommend one. If your top candidate scores below the threshold,
say so and recommend **NO-GO**. Do not inflate a score to keep a slot
filled; there is no slot to fill.

---

## The five dimensions

Score each 0–4. Maximum 20.

### 1. Visual proof in the first second (0–4)

Does the hook land on something the viewer can *see* that they could not
see themselves? The 4D walk-around, a tempo bar, a score appearing on a rep
that looked clean.

- **4** — The first frame is the product doing its trick on a real lift.
- **2** — Real lifting footage, but the product's contribution arrives later.
- **0** — A talking head, a title card, or a stock gym shot.

This dimension is weighted first on purpose. This is a demonstration
channel; a hook that opens on anything but the demonstration is wasting its
most valuable frame.

### 2. Specific, checkable claim (0–4)

- **4** — One concrete, verifiable specific: a named lift, a real number
  from an actual 4Dcoach output, a named lifter.
- **2** — Concrete but soft ("most people miss depth here").
- **0** — Generic hype, or any number you cannot source.

Two or more numerals in the hook scores **0** regardless of accuracy — a
number jumble reads as noise at thumb speed.

### 3. Open loop the video actually closes (0–4)

- **4** — Poses a question the viewer needs answered, and the video answers
  it in the back half.
- **2** — Mild curiosity, or the answer arrives too early to hold anyone.
- **0** — The hook states its own conclusion (nothing left to watch for),
  or promises something the video never delivers.

Overpromising scores 0. Satisfaction-weighted ranking punishes a mismatch
harder than a weak hook ever punishes you.

### 4. Stakes the target viewer already has (0–4)

Target viewer: trains seriously, films sets sometimes, suspects their form
is off but cannot tell from a flat phone video.

- **4** — Names a frustration that viewer has had this month.
- **2** — Real but generic lifting pain point.
- **0** — Stakes only a coach or an engineer would care about.

### 5. Three-second test (0–4)

Read the hook out loud with a stopwatch.

- **4** — Under 3 seconds, one clause, no throat-clearing, and a
  non-specialist gets it in one pass.
- **2** — Slightly long, or needs a beat to parse.
- **0** — Over 4 seconds, or contains jargon ("kinematic", "sagittal",
  "concentric velocity") without instantly showing what it means.

---

## Thresholds

| Total | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 16–20 | **GO** | Strong. Proceed to render after human approval. |
| 13–15 | **BORDERLINE** | Present it, but name the weakest dimension and offer one concrete rewrite. Ali decides. |
| 0–12 | **NO-GO** | Do not proceed. Say what is missing and what footage or product moment would fix it. |

A hook that scores 0 on dimension 1 is **NO-GO regardless of its total**.
There is no such thing as a great hook for this channel that does not open
on the product working.

---

## Scoring honestly

You are scoring your own work, which is exactly where scores drift upward.
Two guards:

1. For each dimension, quote the specific words or the specific visual that
   earn the score. A score you cannot attach to evidence is a 2 at best.
2. State the strongest argument *against* your recommended hook before you
   recommend it.

A rubric that never produces a NO-GO is a rubric that is not being applied.
Roughly one in three candidate sets should fail to produce a GO. If you find
yourself reaching to justify a 16, the honest answer is BORDERLINE.
